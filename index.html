Letters

An attempt to preserve family history. These are the wartime love letters from my grandfather to my grandmother. They follow him through basic training, his arrival at Pearl Harbor months before Japan's attack, and his voyage aboard the U.S.S. New Orleans (CA-32). Due to naval censorship during the war, the story of the New Orleans cannot be ascertained from his writings. However, its absolutely fascinating history earned it nicknames like 'The N.O. (Such) Boat,' 'The Ghost Ship,' and 'The Miracle Ship.' A quote about the ship comes from a captain aboard her during the war, recounted by the ship's chaplain, Howell M. Forgy, in his book 'And Pass the Ammunition':

The [Japanese] have not only declared this ship to be "sunk," but now, since she is proving to be embarrassingly active, they have declared her to be "obsolete!" All she asks is for them to come out of hiding and try to prove that.

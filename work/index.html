Caddy Docs

A Mac app that gives every project a pull-out tab at the edge of the screen: its files, folders, notes, documents and videos, opened when you need them and tucked away when you do not. I designed it, wrote it in Swift, built the site and the App Store listing, and shipped the first version in three and a half weeks. It is on the Mac App Store today, and a French localization followed within days of the first review.

Tools built around a year of litigation

I clerked at a litigation firm after law school. Most of what I built there exists so that the paper can be trusted. None of it is public. All of it is real.

  • Fee audit engine. Four hundred ten billing entries coded into nine categories with one reason per entry; every figure in the brief re-derived by script from the source; a six-gate checker that fails the document if any quotation, number or heading drifts.
  • Verification passes. Independent record, law and skeptic reviews, run by agents against primary sources, before anything is filed.
  • Template builders. Documents built by cloning a firm’s own exemplars, then scrubbed of every trace of metadata.
  • Binder. A Word litigation binder that keeps itself in sync with the file system.
  • Copydesk. Double-click any word on a rendered page and rewrite it at the source.
  • RuleDrill. Typed-recall drills over a hundred and four cited rules, graded.
  • Dictum. Voice memo to transcript to note to reminder, hands free.
  • Notch Ball. A tennis ball that drops out of the MacBook notch. Press a key. It bounces.
